What a cold reader wrote after being given the hand-off page and nothing else.
This is the answer the class keeps beside the submission; cold_reader.py checks
mechanically that it accounts for the whole page, and the teacher reads it.

The program takes one CSV table of student names and whole-number scores -- the
table is a constant inside the program, not a file it opens -- and turns it into
a small report: the class average, the middle score, a letter grade for each of
the twelve students, and a bar chart of how many students got each letter. It
runs as four steps over a store of named values. Parse turns the CSV text into a
roster of name-and-score rows sorted by name, and refuses a table whose header
line is not the expected one. Stats is a single step holding two independent
calculations: Mean gives the average (80.5, reported with the total and the head
count so the division can be redone by hand) and Median gives the middle score
(82.5, the average of the two middle rows because there is an even number of
students); neither reads the other's answer. Letters applies cutoffs that are
passed in as arguments -- A from 90, B from 80, C from 70, D from 60, else F --
to every row, keeping the roster's order. Histogram turns those letters into a
text bar chart in A-to-F order, one hash per student with the count at the end:
3 A, 4 B, 3 C, 1 D, 1 F.

Every calculation leaves a receipt of what it read, what it wrote, how long it
took and a fingerprint of its own source, so the run can be replayed and compared.

The files the page accounts for: homework.py is the program itself; grade.py is
the mechanical rubric of four checks; test_students.py tests both; README.md
explains the idea to a non-programmer; HANDOFF.md is the page I read;
evidence/run-1/record.json is the shipped run; evidence/run-1/receipts.json is
one receipt per calculation of that run; evidence/run-1/tick-viewer.html is the
same run as a page you can step through; and tick_laws.py is named as the check
that reads the record and reports the Stats step doing more work than it takes
time.
